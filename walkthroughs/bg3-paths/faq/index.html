Questions to AskThe questions we wish we'd known to ask

You said you don't know what you don't know. This page is a list of the questions new players should ask, with plain answers, grouped by worry: before you start, characters and choices, fighting, companions, camp and saving, and the story.

Before you start

Do I need to know D&D?

No. The game does the rules and the rolling. The Dice & Numbers page explains the numbers if you're curious.

How long does it take?

A lot. Many players take 75–100 hours or more for one playthrough, and there's no clock. You can stop and start whenever you like.

Will I be bad at it?

Probably not. Fights are turn-based with no time pressure, and Explorer difficulty makes them gentler. Some people play the whole game for the story.

What difficulty should I pick?

Explorer for story-first, Balanced if you like a little challenge. Both can be changed later. Avoid Honour mode.

Should I play a premade character or make my own?

A custom hero is easier for beginners, and our paths are built for them. The Origin characters have their own stories and are great on a second playthrough.

What does my character's appearance affect?

Almost nothing about the rules. Pick whatever you like. Who you can romance doesn't depend on your gender or body.

Characters and choices

What's the difference between race, background and class?

Race is who you are (a human, an elf…). Background is what you did before the game (a soldier, a noble…), and gives you two skills. Class is what you do now (a wizard, a fighter…), and decides your abilities.

Which class should I pick?

Any class can finish the game. For a gentle start, a Fighter, Paladin, Cleric or Barbarian is forgiving. Our path pages pick for you.

What if I pick the wrong build?

You can redo it at camp with Withers for 100 gold per character. You lose the build, not the story.

Do my choices really matter?

Yes, some of them. A few are permanent, and we mark them. A manual save before a big choice lets you try both ways.

Can I play good, evil or in between?

Yes. That's what our paths are for: the Paragon is good, the Absolute is dark, the Sellsword is mercenary.

What does 'point of no return' mean?

A moment after which you can't go back (a door closes for good). The game rarely warns you, so save before big decisions.

Fighting

What happens when a fight starts?

The game switches to turn-based mode. Everyone takes turns, in the order shown across the top. Nothing moves while you think.

What can I do on my turn?

Move, one action, one bonus action, and one reaction on someone else's turn. See How Fights Work.

Why did I miss when it said 75%?

75% means you miss one time in four. It happens. Dice are dice.

What's 'advantage'?

You roll twice and keep the better result. See Advantage and Disadvantage.

What's concentration?

Some spells last as long as you focus on them, and you can only focus on one at a time. Taking damage can break it.

What if my character dies?

They're Downed first, and roll three death saves. Healing gets them back up. If they die, Withers can bring back most companions for 200 gold.

Do I have to kill everything?

No. You can often talk, sneak or skip a fight. You get the same XP for getting past an enemy as for killing it.

Can I run away?

Yes. A character who moves far enough away can leave combat. Use the Disengage action first to avoid free attacks.

Companions and romance

Who should I bring along?

Our path pages recommend a party for each build. A good rule is one fighter, one healer, one damage dealer, plus you.

Can I ignore the companions?

Yes, but you'll miss a lot of the story. See the Lone Wanderer for a no-companions run.

Can I lose a companion?

Yes: death, big story choices, or low approval. Withers can bring most back.

What does approval do?

It tracks how much a companion likes your choices. High approval unlocks their stories and romance. See Approval.

Who can I romance?

Any companion, whatever your character looks like. Each path recommends a male and a female pick, and Beyond the Companions covers six other one-time encounters.

Can I romance more than one person?

For a while, but some companions end a romance if you pursue someone else. Our Charmer's ledger covers who tolerates what.

Camp, saving and items

When should I rest?

Short rest after a hard fight. Long rest when you're out of spells or hurt, or when you want to hear your companions.

What are camp supplies?

Food and drink. A long rest costs 40 (80 on harder modes). Pick up everything edible.

How often should I save?

Before every big choice, before every boss, and at the end of every session.

Where should I put things I'm not using?

Right-click and Send to Camp. Your chest is shared across all camps.

I'm overloaded. What do I do?

Send heavy things to camp and sell junk. The game slows you down over about 80% of your carrying limit.

Is it okay to use guides?

Yes. That's why they exist. If you want to stay spoiler-free, use our spoiler-boxed pages and avoid reading ahead of your act.

What is going on with the story?

What's the tadpole?

A mind flayer parasite in your head. It's the central problem of the game. See The Tadpole in Your Head.

Can I get it removed?

Not easily, and not early. Finding a cure is the first big goal of the game.

Who is the Dream Guardian?

A mysterious figure who visits you in dreams. We won't spoil it. Play to find out.

What's an Origin character?

One of six companions with a fully written story who you can also play as yourself.

What's 'Tav'?

Fans' nickname for your custom hero. It isn't a character you have to use.

Questions You Didn't Know to Ask

Things new players discover too late

  1. "What does this item do?" Hover over it before you equip or sell it.
  2. "Who in my party has the best skill for this?" Switch to that character before a big conversation.
  3. "Am I about to close a door?" Before you leave an area for good, check the journal for unfinished quests.
  4. "Is anyone hurt, hungry or low on spells?" Check your party before a long rest or a big fight.
  5. "Did I talk to everyone at camp?" The story scenes are easy to miss.
  6. "Can I solve this without fighting?" There's usually a way.
  7. "What would my character do?" The game rewards playing someone, not optimising.
