Resting

Short rests and long rests

Short restLong rest
WhereAnywhere, any time you're not in combat.At camp (the tent), when night passes.
How manyTwo between long rests.As many as you have supplies for.
RestoresUp to half your max hit points, plus some class abilities (Fighters' Action Surge and Second Wind, Warlock spell slots, Monks' Ki, Wild Shape, Channel Divinity). Not normal spell slots.Everything: all hit points, all spell slots, and both short-rest charges.
CostsNothing.Camp supplies: 40 on Explorer and Balanced, 80 on Tactician and Honour. Food and drink are supplies.
StoryNothing happens.Many companion scenes and some quest steps happen on a long rest.
No supplies?You can take a partial rest: half your max HP and half your resources back, and it still triggers camp scenes, but it doesn't refill your short-rest charges. Pick up every bit of food you find.
The rule of thumbShort rest after a hard fight. Long rest when you're out of spells, hurt, or want to hear what your companions have to say.

Your Camp

Home base, wherever you are

You can…How
Store thingsThe Traveller's Chest is at every camp, and shares its contents everywhere. Right-click an item to send it to camp from anywhere. Stored items don't weigh you down.
Change your partySwap who's with you at camp. Characters left behind share the XP you earn but not short-rest benefits.
Talk to companionsMost story scenes happen here, especially after a long rest.
See WithersThe skeleton in your camp. See below.
Change how you lookThe Magic Mirror works for custom heroes and hirelings (not for the six Origin characters).
Party sizeYou adventure in a party of four: you plus three companions. The rest wait at camp. You can swap whenever you're at camp.

Withers: Respec, Revive, Hire

The skeleton who runs your safety net

ServiceWhat it doesCost
RespecRe-pick a character's class, subclass, ability scores, skills, spells and feats. It resets them to level 1 so you rebuild their levels. You can't change race, background or looks. Works on companions too.100 gold per character
ReviveBrings back a dead companion (some story-critical deaths excepted).200 gold each
HirelingsHire a mercenary of any class once you reach level 3. One of each of the 12 classes, up to three at a time. They have no personal quests, no approval and no tadpole powers.100 gold each
The biggest reassuranceYou can fix a bad build. If you pick a class and hate it, Withers will let you start that character over for 100 gold. You won't lose the story, only the build.

The Tadpole in Your Head

What it is, and what you can do about it

At the start of the game, mind flayers (tentacled monsters) implant a tadpole in your hero's brain. If it grows, you turn into one of them. That's why the first big goal of the game is to find a cure. Your companions all have one too, so everyone's in the same boat.

For a long stretch of the game the tadpole is a story problem, not a mechanic. The only thing it asks of you is a choice about how much to use its power.

The beginner versionUsing the tadpole's power is optional. Around the Goblin Camp, a dream figure encourages you to accept it, which opens the Illithid Powers skill tree: extra abilities like a psychic blast or a stunning cone. You can accept or decline. Companions mostly go along, but some need convincing.
⚠️ Spoiler: more about tadpole powers

You gain power points from collectible Mind Flayer Parasite Specimens (looted from infected cultists, quests and rewards), and spend them in the Illithid Powers tree. Several powers are 'either-or' branches, so you can't take everything. At the end of Act Two you're offered a special tadpole that makes you a 'partial illithid', which permanently changes how your character looks. In the final act you can go further. We don't know of anything that stops you finishing the game without ever using the powers, but we haven't confirmed it, so treat that as unverified.

Save before this oneThe big tadpole choices change how people talk to you and how some story paths unfold. Make a manual save before accepting, and again before the end of Act Two.
To verify while provingWe couldn't confirm that a hero can finish the game with no tadpole powers at all, or the exact companion reactions to using them. Treat 'refusing everything' as untested.

The Mysterious Artefact

The iron box that's quietly important

Early on a many-sided iron box with glowing runes ends up with your group. Shadowheart carries it, or it lands in a party member's inventory if she isn't there. It can't be opened, can't be dropped, and can't be sold: it's the only item in the game you can never get rid of. You'll learn why it matters as you play.

⚠️ Spoiler: what it does

A vision near the Goblin Camp reveals that the artefact has been protecting your party from the Absolute's influence, a power that can control tadpoled people through their tadpoles. Without it, the party could be dominated.

Approval: Do They Like You?

How companions feel about your choices

ApprovalMeaning
−50 to −20Unhappy. At −20 and −40 you get a warning. At −50 they leave for good and take their equipped gear (the rest of their things come back in a backpack).
−19 to 20Neutral.
21 to 60Friendly to high. Unlocks personal quests and opens up romance.
61 to 100Devoted. The best outcomes for their story.
How to raise itThey react to your choices when they're nearby. Their likes and dislikes are why our Charmer's ledger exists. Charming a companion with a spell costs 10 approval when it wears off, so don't.

You earn XP by defeating or peacefully getting past creatures, discovering places, and completing quest steps. Skipping a fight gives the same XP as winning it. Most XP goes to your whole group, including companions at camp. When you level up, a Level Up button appears under your character's portrait.

Multiclassing in one paragraphOn a level-up you can add a level in a different class instead of advancing your current one. It gives you a taste of the other class's early abilities, but it delays your main class's best features. Our paths don't use it. It's disabled on Explorer difficulty.
To verify while provingThe XP thresholds come from the wiki. Trust the Level Up button in the game over our table if they ever differ.

Items, Weight and Gold

Your backpack

TopicWhat to know
Carrying weightYour limit is about 40 + 10 × your Strength kilograms. Go above 80% and you're Encumbered (slower). Above about 93% you're Heavily Encumbered. Send heavy items to the camp chest.
RarityItems are colour-coded by rarity, from common up to legendary. Hover to see what an item does.
SellingTraders buy at roughly 40% of an item's list price on default settings, and sell at about 250% of it. Explorer is 20% cheaper to buy from, Tactician is worse.
MoneyGold is shared by the whole party and is used for shopping, Withers, and some dialogue.
To verify while provingThe rarity color order and trader prices should be confirmed in the game. The wiki pages for rarity and trading weren't available to read.

Talking, Checks and Stealing

The non-combat toolkit

SituationWhat to know
Dialogue skill checksOptions with a dice icon are checks. The game shows the skill and the target number. A failure usually opens another route. If you have Inspiration, you can re-roll.
Who talks?The character you're controlling does the talking. Before a big conversation, switch to the party member with the best skill for the check you expect (Charisma for Persuasion, and so on).
Locked thingsPicking a lock uses a skill check, plus thieves' tools. A strong character can also break some doors.
Traps and secretsYour passive Perception spots them automatically. A higher Perception score makes you notice more.
StealingPickpocketing is a Sleight of Hand check. If you fail, people notice. Save first.
SneakingStealth is rolled against enemies' passive Perception. In plain view you're spotted instantly.
To verify while provingThe 'switch to the best speaker' tip and the lockpicking tools detail come from general play knowledge, so confirm them in game.

Saving

Never lose your progress

HowWhat to know
AutosaveThe game saves automatically, often.
Manual saveOpen the menu and choose Save. Use it before every big choice.
QuicksaveOn a computer: F5 saves and F8 loads. Check the Options screen for the Xbox equivalent.
Honour modeOne save only. It overwrites itself. Avoid it for now.
Name your savesSomething like "Before the Grove raid" will save you hours of confusion later.
To verify while provingThe wiki's saving page wasn't available, so confirm autosave frequency and the Xbox quicksave button in your game.

Playing Together

You and your wife

The game supports co-op, including on one screen. We haven't tested how romances and party choices work when two people share one story, and the wiki pages we read don't say. Our advice for now is two separate saves, one path each, so each of you gets a full story. Try co-op afterwards, and tell us what you find.
