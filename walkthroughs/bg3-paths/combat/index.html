How Fights WorkTurns, actions, surfaces and spells

A fight in this game is a turn-based puzzle, not a test of reflexes. This page explains the two modes, what you can do on a turn, how the numbers become hits and misses, and the surfaces, conditions and spells that make fights interesting.

The one-sentence versionA fight is a board game: everyone takes turns in a set order, and on your turn you can move, do one main thing, and one smaller thing. Nothing happens until you decide.

Two Modes: Exploring and Fighting

How combat starts and ends

Most of the game is real time: you walk, talk and look around. When a fight starts (you attack, you're spotted, or the story triggers one), the game switches to turn-based mode automatically and everyone rolls initiative. When the fight ends, it switches back. You can also switch it on yourself outside a fight.

In turn-based mode nothing moves while you think. Take as long as you like. When you're done with a character's turn, press End Turn (the Y button on Xbox).

In plain EnglishIf you walk into a room and everyone suddenly stops and a row of portraits appears at the top, that's combat. Don't panic: you're in the board game now.

What You Can Do On Your Turn

Four things, refreshed every turn

WhatHow manyExamples
MovementA set distance (normal is about 9 m / 30 ft). Dashing doubles it.Walking, running to cover, climbing a ladder.
ActionOne per turn.Attack, cast most spells, Dash, Disengage, Help a friend, Throw something.
Bonus actionOne per turn.Jump, Shove, off-hand attack, quick spells like Healing Word. Rogues can Dash, Disengage or Hide as a bonus action.
ReactionOne per round, used on someone else's turn.A free attack on an enemy who runs past you, or a spell like Shield or Counterspell.
Extra AttackMany classes get Extra Attack at level 5: your Attack action gives two attacks instead of one. A level 11 Fighter gets three.
Don't panic about reactionsYou can set each reaction to "auto" or "ask me" in the Reactions tab (key L on a computer). If you're unsure, leave them on "ask."

Initiative and Turn Order

Who goes first

At the start of a fight everyone rolls initiative: a d4 plus their Dexterity modifier. The portraits along the top show the order. Friends who are next to each other in the order take their turns together. If you're surprised, you can't act or react for the whole first round, which is why sneaking up on enemies is so strong.

Hitting and Being Hit

Hit chance, armor and damage

When you attack, the game shows a hit chance as a percentage. It's built from the numbers on the Dice & Numbers page: your d20 plus your bonuses against the enemy's armor class. A hit rolls damage dice. A natural 20 always hits and doubles the dice. A natural 1 always misses.

TipWhy
Attack from high ground.Attacking from about 2.5 m above gets +2 to hit. Attacking from below gets −2.
Avoid shooting when an enemy is next to you.A ranged attack with an enemy adjacent has disadvantage.
Use surprise.Sneak up and attack first. Hidden attackers get advantage.
Attack helpless enemies in melee.Sleeping, paralysed or downed targets take automatic critical hits.
Leaving a fightIf you move away from an enemy who is next to you, they get a free attack (an opportunity attack). The Disengage action prevents that for the turn.

The Ground Is Part of the Fight

Fire, water, oil, ice and what happens when they meet

SurfaceWhat it does
FireBurning does 1d4 fire damage each turn.
WaterMakes creatures Wet. Cold turns it to ice, lightning electrifies it, and fire turns it to steam.
IceMakes creatures fall prone, and is difficult to walk on.
Electrified water1d4 lightning damage each turn.
Oil, alcohol, smokepowderExplode into fire when lit. Be careful where you stand.
GreaseMakes you fall over.
AcidLowers armor class by 2.
Webs and vinesHold creatures in place.
Poison1d4 poison damage each round.
Classic combosWater + lightning is a big shock for everyone standing in it. Oil + any fire is a bonfire. Ice + fire becomes water. Flying creatures avoid ground surfaces. A surface doesn't hurt someone already standing in it until they move.

Spells in a Fight

Cantrips, slots and concentration

Cantrips are free spells you can cast any number of times. Spell slots are limited: a leveled spell spends a slot of that level or higher, and most casters get them back on a long rest (Warlocks on a short rest). Concentration means you can only hold one lasting spell at a time, and taking damage can break it.

Keeping concentrationWhen you're hit, you roll a Constitution save against 10 or half the damage, whichever is higher. It also ends if you're knocked prone, put to sleep or downed. To drop a spell yourself, use the X on its icon (on Xbox, open the action wheel and hold Y).

When Someone Falls

Downed, death saves, and getting up

What happensDetails
At 0 HP you're Downed.You can't act. You roll one death save each turn.
Death saving throwA plain d20 against DC 10. Three successes and you're stable. Three failures and you're dead. A natural 1 counts as a failure.
Taking damage while downedEach hit counts as a failed save. A critical hit counts as two. Melee hits on a downed target are always critical hits.
Getting back upAny healing or a friend's Help action gets you up. That turn you can only use bonus actions.
Bringing back the deadRevivify (a spell), a Scroll of Revivify, or Withers at camp for 200 gold. Some story-critical deaths can't be undone.

Difficulty Modes

What each setting actually changes

ModeWhat changes
ExplorerEnemies have 30% less health, your party has double health, merchants are 20% cheaper, and everyone gets a bigger proficiency bonus. Multiclassing is disabled. Best for story-first newcomers.
BalancedThe game as designed.
TacticianEnemies have more health, smarter AI, +2 to attack and spell DCs, and some get extra abilities. Long rests cost twice as many camp supplies, and merchants charge more.
HonourTactician plus boss tricks, and a single save file that overwrites itself. If your party wipes, you choose: switch to a custom mode or end the campaign and delete the save. For veterans only.
CustomPick your own rules: camp cost, death saves, trade prices and more.
Our adviceStart on Explorer or Balanced. You can change them from the pause menu (Honour mode can't be changed back). Nobody is keeping score.
