Chapter 1

The Idea

Who the Paragon is, and why a Paladin

The Paragon always does the right thing, even when it costs them. They protect the weak, keep their word, and never take what isn't theirs. A Paladin is built for exactly that: durable, hits hard, heals allies with a touch, and gets stronger the more charming they are, because Charisma powers their special auras.

It's also a forgiving class for a first-time D&D player. You wear the best armor, you carry a shield, and you're hard to kill in the first acts.

Patch 8 alternativePatch 8 added the Oath of the Crown, a Paladin oath about duty and protecting others. If "noble guardian" appeals more than "holy champion," try it. We built this page around Devotion because it's the classic choice.

Level by Level

What to pick at each level, and what you're getting

LevelWhat you getWhat to do
1Lay on Hands (a pool of healing you can spend on anyone), Divine SenseUse Lay on Hands to heal between fights.
2Divine Smite, Fighting Style, spellsPick Defense. Smite burns a spell slot for extra holy damage on a hit: save it for tough enemies.
3Oath of Devotion, Channel OathTake Devotion. Sacred Weapon adds your Charisma to your attacks for a while.
4Ability Improvement or feat+1 Strength, +1 Charisma; or take Sentinel or Tough.
5Extra Attack, second-level spellsYou now hit twice. Your damage jumps.
6Aura of ProtectionThe big one: you and nearby allies add your Charisma bonus to saving throws. Keep your friends close.
7Aura of DevotionAllies near you can't be charmed.
8Ability ImprovementMore Strength or Charisma.
9Third-level spellsRevivify (bring back a fallen ally) arrives.
10Aura of CourageAllies near you can't be frightened.
11Improved Divine SmiteEvery melee hit now carries a little extra holy damage.
12Final Ability ImprovementYou're done. Max level.
Stay one classDon't multiclass on your first Paragon run. A pure Paladin is simple, strong and thematic.

Staying on Target

The Paragon's Playthrough

A checklist of in-character choices act by act. Each is a step you can tick off. If you miss one, the page tells you how to repair it.

Act One

Act One: Be the Hero

The choices that define a good character in the wilderness

  1. Escape the Nautiloid and help your fellow prisoners rather than only yourself.
  2. At the Emerald Grove, fight on the tieflings' side against the goblins.
  3. Recruit Wyll to your party.
  4. Save Arabella: talk Kagha into freeing her.
  5. Never take the Sacred Idol. It's a quick way to doom the tieflings.
  6. Rescue Halsin from the Goblin Camp, and stop the Rite of Thorns.
  7. Free Mayrina from the hag, and refuse her bargain.
  8. Side with Karlach over the paladins at the Toll House.
  9. Put out the fire at Waukeen's Rest before you rest or travel.
Don't undo yourselfA single wrong click at the Grove, like taking the Idol or attacking a druid, can lock out the Paragon's best Act One endings. Save before every big choice.
Recommended: your first big findHead to the Rosymorn Monastery Trail's puzzle for the Blood of Lathander. It's the best weapon a Paragon can have this early.

Act Two

Act Two: Hold the Line

The Shadow-Cursed Lands test a Paragon's patience

  1. Rescue the Moonrise prisoners; don't walk past them to save time.
  2. At Shar's trial, spare and free the Nightsong with Shadowheart in the party. That's the choice that earns the Spear of Night.
  3. Spend Revivify and healing freely on allies rather than stockpiling it.
  4. Choose mercy where the game offers it, and see what it costs.
To verify while provingReplace with the exact Act Two quests after playing.
Act Three

Act Three: The Right Ending

The city, the Elder Brain, and your final choice

  1. Help the people of Baldur's Gate even when they can't pay you.
  2. Follow Wyll's story to its end: it's a path to Balduran's Giantslayer.
  3. Keep your promises to companions, and see their final chapters through.
  4. At the finale, take the ending that fits who you've been all game.
