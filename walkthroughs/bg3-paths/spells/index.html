Magic in Five Minutes

What a new player needs to know

  1. Cantrips are free. They cost no spell slot, so use them as often as you like. Every caster has a few.
  2. Spell slots are limited. A leveled spell spends a slot, and most casters get them back on a long rest. Warlocks get theirs back on a short rest, which is why they love resting.
  3. Concentration means one at a time. Spells marked as needing concentration end if you cast another one, or if you take a bad hit.
  4. Reactions happen on the enemy's turn. Shield and Counterspell are reactions, so keep a slot free for them.
  5. Some classes prepare, some know. Clerics, Druids, Paladins and Wizards choose spells to prepare after a rest. Bards, Sorcerers and Warlocks have a fixed list that only changes when they level.
When to spend slotsA good rule for a first run: cantrips for ordinary fights, slots for bosses and crowds, and always one slot held back for emergencies.

Wizards and Scrolls

How to grow a spellbook

  1. A Wizard learns spells by copying scrolls: right-click the scroll and choose Learn Spell, or use Learn More Spells in the spellbook.
  2. Each copy costs 50 gold per spell level, and the Wizard needs a spell slot of that level.
  3. A Wizard pays half price for spells from their own school. (Bladesingers don't get the discount.)
  4. Only Wizard-list spells can be copied, and a few have no scroll at all. Counterspell, Shield, Find Familiar and Toll the Dead only come from leveling up.
  5. Scrolls of Revivify and Speak with Dead can't be copied: they aren't on the Wizard list.
How many spells you end up withAt level 12: a Wizard's spellbook holds about 28 spells; a Bard knows 15; a Sorcerer or Warlock knows 13. Cleric, Druid and Paladin prepare from their lists each day.
