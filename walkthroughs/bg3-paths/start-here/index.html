What Is This Game?

The short version

Baldur's Gate 3 is a single-player story game (it also has co-op) where you create a hero and travel with a small band of companions across three big chapters, called acts. You talk to people, explore, fight, make choices, and rest at a camp. A full playthrough takes a long time: many players report 75 to 100 hours or more, and there's no hurry.

It's a role-playing game (an RPG). That means the story changes depending on what you decide and how well your character does at things. Most of the game is conversation and exploring. The fights are puzzles you solve one move at a time.

Your hero has a mind flayer tadpole in their head. That's the engine of the story, and the game sets it up right away. You don't need to understand it yet: the Systems page explains it when you're ready.

No stopwatchUnlike a lot of games, there's no daily clock. Almost nothing happens because time passes. A few scripted moments are timed, and the game tells you when. Take your time.

What Is D&D, Really?

The 60-second version for a non-nerd

Dungeons & Dragons is a tabletop game where a group sits around a table and tells a story together. One person, the Dungeon Master (DM), describes the world. Everyone else plays one character. When a player says "I try to jump the gap," and the outcome isn't certain, they roll a twenty-sided die. Add your character's skill, compare to a target number, and the result tells the story what happens.

That's the whole idea. Baldur's Gate 3 is that game on a computer: the game is the Dungeon Master, and it rolls the dice for you. You can watch the dice if you like. The numbers on your character sheet are the same ones a tabletop player would use.

In plain EnglishThink of it like Stardew Valley's fishing minigame or a mine fight: your skill level quietly changes your odds. Here the odds are written on the screen as numbers, and a die gets rolled in the open instead of being hidden.

How the Game Flows

The loop you'll repeat for the whole game

StepWhat you're doingWhat it feels like
1. ExploreWalk around in real time, look at things, pick things up, open doors.Like walking around your farm, but with more things that want to talk to you.
2. TalkChoose what to say. Some choices show a dice icon: that's a skill check.Like choosing gift or dialogue options in Stardew, except the choices actually change the story.
3. Fight (sometimes)The game switches to turn-based: everyone gets a turn, one at a time.Like a board game. Nothing happens until you decide. You can think as long as you like.
4. Loot and sellTake what you find. Trade for better gear.Like the mines: you find stuff, you sell stuff, you upgrade.
5. Rest at campHeal, recover spells, talk to your companions, swap party members.Like going home and sleeping. Many of the best story scenes happen here.
6. Level upPick a new ability when you gain a level.Like levelling a skill, except you choose what you get.
RepeatDo it again in a new place with a new problem.Each act ends with a big turning point.
The three chaptersAct One is the wilderness, the Emerald Grove and the Goblin Camp. Act Two is the shadow-cursed lands and Moonrise Towers. Act Three is the city of Baldur's Gate and the finale. Our path pages walk through each one step by step.

Your First Hour, Step by Step

What to do, in order

  1. Choose a difficulty. If you're here for the story, pick Explorer: enemies have less health, your party has more, and shopping is cheaper. You can change it later from the pause menu. (Honour mode can't be changed, so stay away from it on a first run.)
  2. Choose who to be. You can play your own custom hero or one of the six Origin characters. A custom hero is easier for beginners, and our path pages are built for them.
  3. Build your hero. Pick a race, background and class, then spread points across six abilities. If that sounds like homework, copy one of our path builds. You pick how your hero looks.
  4. Wake up on the ship. The opening chapter, the Nautiloid, is a guided escape. Look around, talk to everyone, and don't worry about doing it perfectly.
  5. Meet your first companions. They join you in the first areas after the crash. You'll have a party of up to four: you plus three.
  6. Take your first fight slowly. When combat starts, the game pauses between turns. Read the tooltips. Hover over anything to see what it does.
  7. Make camp and take a long rest. Talk to every companion. This is where the story deepens.
  8. Save. Open the menu and make a manual save, then do it again before every big choice.

Seven Habits That Make It Easier

Not rules. Just things that help

  1. Talk to everyone, and read everything. Most of the best moments are conversations.
  2. Hover over things. The game has tooltips for nearly everything, including exactly what your chances are.
  3. Save before big choices, and name your saves. "Before the Grove" beats "Quicksave 14."
  4. Rest often. Many companion scenes only appear after a long rest. Short rests are free and help a lot between fights.
  5. A failed roll isn't a failure. Most of the time a failed check opens a different path instead of ending the story.
  6. You don't have to fight everything. Many fights can be skipped, talked out of, or avoided with stealth.
  7. It's okay to look things up. That's why these pages exist.

What Can I Actually Mess Up?

Honest answers, so you don't have to worry

WorryHonest answer
Will I break the game?Almost certainly not. The game is built so you can make choices, fail rolls, and still keep going.
Can I lose a companion?Yes. They can die in a fight, leave if they dislike you enough, or get locked out by a big story choice. Withers, at camp, can bring back most who die, for gold.
Can I miss quests?Yes. Some close when the story moves on. We mark the big ones in the path guides.
Is there a wrong class?No. Every class can finish the game. Some are easier, and our paths say which.
Will I get stuck?Rarely. If you do, our step-by-step routes and the FAQ are here to help.
The best safety netA manual save before a big choice. If you don't like what happens, load it and try the other way.
