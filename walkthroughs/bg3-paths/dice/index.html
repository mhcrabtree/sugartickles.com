The one idea that explains everythingWhenever the outcome is uncertain, the game rolls a twenty-sided die (a d20), adds your bonuses, and compares the total to a target number. Equal or higher wins. Everything on this page is a variation on that.

Your Six Abilities

The six numbers on your character sheet

AbilityWhat it meansWhat it's for
StrengthRaw muscle.Melee attacks and damage, jumping, carrying, throwing and shoving (the Athletics skill).
DexteritySpeed and balance.Ranged attacks, finesse weapons, armor class (up to a cap), initiative, and the Acrobatics, Sleight of Hand and Stealth skills.
ConstitutionToughness.Hit points each level, and resisting poison and keeping a spell going when you're hit. No skills.
IntelligenceBook smarts.Wizard-type casting, and the Arcana, History, Investigation, Nature and Religion skills.
WisdomAwareness and common sense.Cleric, Druid, Monk and Ranger casting, and the Animal Handling, Insight, Medicine, Perception and Survival skills. Wisdom saves are very common.
CharismaForce of personality.Barbarian, Bard, Paladin, Sorcerer and Warlock casting, and the Deception, Intimidation, Performance and Persuasion skills. It also affects trader prices.
Why we always tell you the main twoEvery class has one or two abilities that do most of the work. Our path pages tell you which to raise, so you never have to guess.

Modifiers: the Number That Actually Gets Added

A score is the big number. The modifier is the small one that matters

Your ability score (like 16) isn't what you add to a roll. You add the modifier, which comes from the score:

modifier = (score − 10) ÷ 2, rounded down

ScoreModifierScoreModifier
8–9−118–19+4
10–11+020–21+5
12–13+122–23+6
14–15+224–25+7

A shortcutEach time your score goes up by 2, the modifier goes up by 1. That's why Ability Improvements at level 4, 8 and 12 are so valuable: +2 to a score is +1 on everything it touches.
Limits to knowWhen you create your hero, every score starts between 8 and 15 before bonuses. Feats and level-ups can't raise a score past 20, though items can push it higher.

Proficiency Bonus: Being Trained

A second bonus for things you're good at

Your levelProficiency bonus
1–4+2
5–8+3
9–12+4

You add your proficiency bonus when you roll something you've been trained in. That's:

  1. Skills you're proficient in (the game marks them on your sheet).
  2. Attacks with weapons you're trained with, and all spell attacks.
  3. Saving throws your class is trained in (every class has two).
  4. Your spell save DC (see below).
Expertise and Jack of All TradesExpertise (Rogues and Bards get it) doubles your proficiency bonus on a skill. A Bard's Jack of All Trades adds half of it to skills they aren't trained in.
Wrong armorIf you wear armor or hold a shield you aren't trained with, you get disadvantage on checks, saves and attacks, and you can't cast spells.

The Rolls, One by One

Every kind of roll you'll see, and what it's compared to

RollWhat you rollCompared toWhere you see it
Attack rolld20 + ability modifier + proficiency (if trained) + other bonuses.The target's Armor Class (AC). Equal or higher hits.Every weapon or spell attack. The game shows your hit chance as a percentage.
Damage rollThe weapon's or spell's damage dice, plus your modifier (weapons).Nothing. It's how much damage you deal.After a hit. A critical hit doubles the dice.
Saving throwd20 + ability modifier + proficiency (if your class is trained in it).A Difficulty Class (DC) set by the spell, trap or monster.When something happens to you: a fireball, poison, a charm spell. A success usually halves the damage or avoids the effect.
Ability or skill checkd20 + ability modifier + proficiency (if trained; doubled with expertise).A DC the game sets.Dialogue ("Persuasion DC 12"), lockpicking, spotting traps, jumping, shoving.
Initiatived4 + Dexterity modifier.Everyone else's initiative.At the start of a fight. Highest goes first.
Death saving throwA plain d20, no bonuses.DC 10.When you're at 0 HP. Three successes and you're stable. Three failures and you're dead.
Passive check10 + the skill modifier (no roll).Used automatically.Spotting hidden things, or hiding from enemies. The game rolls nothing, it just compares.
AC in one lineArmor Class = 10 + your Dexterity modifier + your armor + your shield + other bonuses. Medium armor caps how much Dexterity helps (+2); heavy armor ignores Dexterity. Some classes have unarmored versions: a Barbarian uses Dexterity and Constitution, a Monk uses Dexterity and Wisdom, and Mage Armour gives 13 + Dexterity. The game uses whichever is highest.
Spell save DC8 + your proficiency bonus + your spellcasting modifier. It's the number enemies have to beat to resist your spells. Open your spellbook (key K on a computer) to see yours.

What each one does, in plain English

AbilitySkills

The ones to care about mostPersuasion, Deception, Intimidation and Insight open dialogue options. Perception spots traps and secrets. Sleight of Hand and Athletics open locks and doors. You don't need all of them, but someone in your party should have each one.

Advantage and Disadvantage

The most useful idea in the whole game

Advantage: roll two d20 and keep the higher. Disadvantage: roll two and keep the lower. It works on attacks, saves and checks, never on damage. Advantage is worth about +3 on an average roll.

They don't stack. If you have advantage from three places, it's still just advantage. If you have one of each, they cancel out and you roll normally.

You get advantage when…You get disadvantage when…
The target is prone, paralysed, asleep, blinded, restrained or entangled.You make a ranged attack while an enemy is right next to you.
You attack while hidden or invisible.You make a ranged attack beyond your weapon's normal range.
Your ally casts Guiding Bolt or Faerie Fire on the target (spells that help your next attack).You attack in heavy darkness and can't see in the dark.
To verify while provingConfirm the exact advantage and disadvantage sources on our version. The first two rows of each list are confirmed. The spell examples (Guiding Bolt, Faerie Fire) are our own examples of 'helping' effects.

Critical Hits and Misses

What a natural 20 and a natural 1 do

RollOn an attackOn other rolls
Natural 20 (the die shows 20)Always hits, and doubles your damage dice.Counts as a critical success on ability checks and dialogue rolls.
Natural 1 (the die shows 1)Always misses.Counts as a critical failure on ability checks and dialogue rolls, and always fails a death save.
Guaranteed critsA melee hit on a sleeping, paralysed or downed enemy is automatically a critical hit. That's why sleep and hold spells are so strong.
Luck evens outThe game uses Karmic Dice by default, which quietly soften long streaks of bad luck on your attacks, enemy saves and dialogue checks.
To verify while provingThe wiki's pages disagree on whether a natural 1 or 20 always matters on saving throws. Treat attacks, checks, concentration saves and dialogue as the ones we're sure about.

Inspiration: A Re-Roll

A small rescue button

You earn Inspiration by doing things that fit your background (soldiers get it for soldier-like deeds, and so on). The party shares up to four points. You can spend one to re-roll a failed d20 roll shown in the roll menu, mostly dialogue checks, lockpicking and traps. You can keep re-rolling until you succeed or run out. It doesn't work on combat attacks or saves.

To verify while provingConfirm how Inspiration is displayed on the controller and whether it works on every dialogue check.

Three Examples, Start to Finish

Reading the numbers

SituationThe mathResult
Attack: A level 1 Fighter (+3 Strength, +2 proficiency) swings at a goblin with AC 15.d20 roll of 11, +3 +2 = 16. Compare to AC 15.16 ≥ 15, hit. Then roll damage.
Saving throw: A goblin shaman casts a spell with DC 13. Your character has +1 in that ability and no proficiency.d20 roll of 10, +1 = 11. Compare to DC 13.11 < 13, failed. You take the full effect.
Dialogue check: Persuasion DC 12. Your character has +2 Charisma and +2 proficiency.d20 roll of 8, +2 +2 = 12. Compare to DC 12.12 ≥ 12, success. Equal counts.
In plain EnglishIf a number on the screen confuses you, hover over it. The game shows exactly where each bonus came from.
